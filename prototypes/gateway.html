BY · Phil Harrison FILED · READ · 4 min
§ 01

Compute it, don't assert it.

A risk weight cross-checked against the regulator's worked examples; a session total calculated by the application rather than taken on trust from a language model. If you can't see how the number was produced, nobody should be relying on it — least of all you.

§ 02

What the tooling costs, someone downstream pays.

A bank pays a vendor for compliance computation and recovers it through the product. A swimming club pays £40 a month for software out of the same pot as the pool time. Push the cost curve down and the saving lands somewhere real.

§ 03

Sustainable software, sustainable teams.

Agile is not a ceremony — it's a way to ship for years without burning out. Small batches, honest tests, and code people are proud to maintain at 11pm on a Tuesday.

§ 04

Constraints are a spec, not an adversary.

The PRA rulebook is an interface. So is a coach on poolside with wet hands and thirty seconds between sets. Read the constraint properly, write tests against it, and most of the design falls out on its own.

Feature · Live · gooseup / thecoachesapp

Training Den

A coach-first session planner for British swimming clubs — describe a session out loud, get a structured one back, and never take a total on trust.

  • Voice to structure. Describe the set on the way to the pool; get back an editable, structured draft.
  • Computed, not guessed. The application calculates every distance — the model is never allowed to assert one.
  • Review before save. Each draft shows its assumptions and flags its ambiguities before anything is written.
  • Affordable by design. Built for volunteer-led clubs — AI cost per session is logged, capped, and kept honest.
